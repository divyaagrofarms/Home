Balanced Pig Nutrition

A successful pig feeding program balances energy, protein, amino acids, minerals, vitamins, water and feed intake according to animal age, weight and production stage.

Energy Growth & maintenance
Protein Muscle development
Minerals Bone & metabolism
Water Essential daily nutrient

Nutrition by Production Stage

Nutritional requirements change significantly as pigs move from newborn piglets through nursery, growing, finishing and breeding stages.

Piglet Nutrition

Early nutrition supports gut development, immunity and healthy early growth.

  • Highly digestible ingredients
  • Quality protein sources
  • Balanced amino acids
  • Clean drinking water

Weaner Nutrition

Post-weaning diets should support feed intake, digestive adaptation and steady growth.

  • Gradual feed transition
  • Digestible energy
  • Essential amino acids
  • Fresh water availability

Grower Nutrition

Growing pigs need balanced nutrition for efficient lean tissue development.

  • Adequate energy
  • Balanced lysine
  • Mineral balance
  • Consistent feeding

Finisher Nutrition

Finishing diets are designed around growth efficiency, carcass targets and feed conversion.

  • Controlled energy
  • Appropriate protein
  • Amino acid balance
  • Feed wastage control

Sow Nutrition

Sow diets need to support body condition, reproduction, pregnancy and milk production.

  • Gestation feeding
  • Lactation nutrition
  • Mineral support
  • Adequate water

Boar Nutrition

Breeding boars require nutrition that maintains body condition, activity and reproductive performance.

  • Controlled body condition
  • Quality protein
  • Vitamins & minerals
  • Consistent water supply

Practical Feed Nutrition Guide

Example production-stage framework. Actual formulations should be adjusted according to genetics, environment, feed ingredients, laboratory analysis and farm performance.

Weaners Rapid adaptation & growth Digestible energy and amino acids Controlled transition ADG, intake, gut health
Growers Lean tissue growth Energy/protein balance Consistent daily feeding Body weight, FCR
Finishers Efficient market growth Energy, amino acids Minimize feed wastage ADG, FCR, body condition
Gestating Sows Pregnancy & body condition Energy, fibre, minerals Condition-based feeding Body condition score
Lactating Sows Milk production & litter growth Energy, protein, amino acids Encourage intake Feed intake, litter growth
Breeding Boars Body condition & reproduction Protein, minerals, vitamins Avoid excessive condition Weight & breeding activity
The table is a management framework rather than a universal feed formulation. Nutrient specifications should be set by a qualified animal nutritionist using the pig genotype, stage, environment and actual ingredient analysis.

Essential Nutrients for Pigs

A complete diet needs multiple nutrient groups. No single ingredient can supply everything required.

Energy

Supports maintenance, movement, growth and reproductive functions.

Protein

Supplies amino acids required for tissue formation and repair.

Amino Acids

Lysine, methionine, threonine and others support protein synthesis.

Minerals

Calcium, phosphorus, sodium and trace minerals support body functions.

Vitamins

Support metabolism, immunity, growth and reproduction.

Water

Essential for digestion, thermoregulation and physiological functions.

Fibre

Supports digestive function and can influence gut health and satiety.

Feed Additives

Selected additives can support feed quality and production objectives.

Common Feed Ingredients

Ingredients should be evaluated for nutrient composition, quality, digestibility, storage condition and cost before inclusion.

Maize / Corn

Primarily used as an energy ingredient. Quality and moisture should be checked before use.

Soybean Meal

A major plant protein source that contributes essential amino acids to balanced pig diets.

Wheat / Grains

Can provide energy and nutrients depending on grain type, quality and diet formulation.

Mineral Mixture

Supplies selected macro and trace minerals according to the complete diet specification.

Rice Bran

Can contribute energy, fibre and other nutrients. Quality varies significantly between sources.

Vitamin Premix

Used to provide vitamins at appropriate inclusion rates in complete diets.

Bran / Fibre Sources

Fibre ingredients may influence gut function, feed intake and diet characteristics.

Specialty Ingredients

Specialty ingredients should be evaluated based on nutritional value and economic return.

Water Management

Water is one of the most important nutrients in a pig production system.

Water Quality

Water should be clean, accessible and monitored regularly. Poor water quality can reduce intake and negatively affect production.

  • Clean drinking points
  • Regular water-line inspection
  • Check for blocked nipples
  • Monitor water cleanliness

Water & Environment

Water demand can increase with temperature, body size, feed intake, lactation and environmental conditions.

  • Increase attention during hot weather
  • Ensure easy access
  • Monitor drinking behaviour
  • Maintain water equipment

Feed Performance Monitoring

Nutrition should be evaluated using actual farm performance rather than feed quantity alone.

ADG

Average Daily Gain

Tracks average body-weight gain per day and helps evaluate growth performance.

FCR

Feed Conversion Ratio

Compares feed consumed with body-weight gain and is an important efficiency indicator.

FI

Feed Intake

Monitoring intake helps identify changes caused by health, environment or feed quality.

Nutrition Warning Signs

Changes in performance or appearance can have many causes. These signs should trigger investigation, not automatic diagnosis.

Reduced Growth

Review feed intake, health, environment, feed quality and nutrient balance.

Reduced Feed Intake

Check water, temperature, feed freshness, health status and feeding management.

Changes in Manure

Investigate diet changes, ingredient quality, gut health and management factors.

Poor Body Condition

Review feeding level, nutrient density, health and stage-specific requirements.

Professional Farm Nutrition Management

A good nutrition program is not only about the formula. Ingredient quality, storage, feed mixing, feeding equipment, water availability, animal health and regular performance measurement all contribute to successful pig production.

Ingredient Testing Check actual ingredient quality.
Accurate Mixing Maintain consistent feed batches.
Storage Control Protect feed from moisture and contamination.
Performance Records Monitor growth and feed efficiency.
Water Monitoring Ensure reliable clean water access.
Health Coordination Coordinate nutrition with herd health.
Important: Nutrient requirements and feed formulations vary with genetics, body weight, production stage, climate, ingredient composition, health status and management system. This webpage is an educational farm-management reference and should not replace a formulation prepared by a qualified animal nutritionist.
